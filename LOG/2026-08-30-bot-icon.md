# 3927d87 — [bot-icon] processed and cleared: the icon settled by rendering it rather than describing it, and the Noun Project credit placed on the sign page

The item had been sitting in Unprocessed since 2026-08-26 with two open
questions, and neither could be answered by reasoning about them.

The first was whether the cat becomes a white silhouette or is redrawn as
line-work. The reference sibling icon is white line-work with no fill and the
source cat is a solid filled silhouette, so the two could not simply be matched.
Six treatments were rendered against the real gradient in a scratch page and
looked at. The first pass framed the cat inside the circle with a rectangular
crop, which was wrong: the user's point was that the circle's own edge should do
the cropping, letting the drawing sit much larger in frame. Redrawn that way, the
user chose the solid silhouette at the larger scale. The defeated option was
converting the cat to line-work by stroking the edge of the filled artwork — it
loses at Discord's real display sizes, and the face details come out as thin
doubled outlines rather than drawn strokes, so a genuine line-work version would
mean redrawing the cat rather than converting it. The user also asked for the
drawing's smaller pieces to be left in place.

That second exchange is worth recording as a miss on this session's part. When
the user asked for the leaves to be removed, the artwork's seven pieces were
colour-coded with a numbered legend so they could name which ones to drop. They
could not tell the colours apart well enough to answer, and the answer that came
back was to leave them. A diagnostic that asks someone to match seven similar
swatches against seven shapes was the wrong instrument, and the session should
have reached for something else.

The second question was attribution. A lookup established that a free Noun
Project download is CC BY 3.0 and obliges a credit wherever the artwork is used,
that cropping the baked-in credit text out of the file does not discharge it, and
that a one-off paid download removes the obligation for about five dollars. The
finding is filed at `resources/research/noun-project-icon-licence.md` with its
index line. The user chose the free licence with the credit placed on the sign
page — the one surface other people see, and one that already credits the
photographers. Buying the licence lost because it spends money to avoid writing a
single line on a page that already carries credits.

Two capability checks ran with the user present. Discord will not take an SVG
avatar, so a raster export is needed: there is no cairosvg and no ImageMagick on
this machine, but Inkscape 1.4.4 is installed and was confirmed working, so the
build has a route to a 512-pixel PNG. And the sign page's credit bar turned out
to be a single element whose contents are rewritten on every photo change, so the
artist credit cannot live inside it and has to be its own static line — a fact
read off the file rather than assumed.

Setting the avatar in the Discord developer portal is not something this session
can reach, so it was split out as [bot-icon-upload] with its own walkthrough and
held against [bot-icon].

SPEC gained a sentence at the decision step rather than at the build: its "the
photo is the point" principle now says the sign page carries a standing credit to
the cat drawing's artist. Product truth was written with the user present, ahead
of the work.
