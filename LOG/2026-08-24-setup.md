# [HASH] — Adopted the AFK-cats folder and scoped it as a Discord-triggered AFK sign

The folder held one file: a self-contained HTML page showing a full-screen AFK
sign — big AFK, a return time, a live countdown — over slowly crossfading cat
photos from Pexels. Setup started from that page and asked what it was for.

The answer moved the project well past the page. Alex wants a Discord bot on
their Throughliner server so the sign is one click away and takes over what they
are screen-sharing until they stop it, with `/afk 3pm` to put it up and `/back`
to take it down.

One thing needed correcting before anything else could be described. Alex's first
framing was that the bot "replaces my stream". A bot runs server-side and cannot
reach into a machine to change what a screen share is showing, so the shape that
actually works is: the bot flips a local AFK screen on and off while Alex keeps
sharing. Alex agreed, and SPEC records that shape.

The late behaviour was Alex's own requirement and is the sharpest thing in the
spec: the countdown must not stop or congratulate you at zero. It flips to
counting up, so the sign shows how overdue you are and keeps climbing until
`/back`. Alex asked for standard colours throughout — being late changes the
numbers, not the look.

Anyone on the server can set their own sign. That collides with the fact that the
sign runs on a person's own machine, which was raised during setup and
deliberately left undecided rather than settled in an interview; it went into the
queue as its own capture for planning.

The existing HTML page was left untouched. It is the source material and the
starting point for the sign, not something setup reorganised.

Alex chose to keep all Throughliner documents tracked in git, and accepted the
Throughliner Brevity output style for this project.
